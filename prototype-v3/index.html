    Product case study · built with the 7-stage "big idea" method

    Where KnowSpot wins in the AI era

    Google now answers questions about places. A dozen apps turn saved reels into maps. Nobody verifies what is happening on your street today. This page is the evidence, the bet, and how I'd know if I'm wrong.

    The 2-minute version (say this in the interview)

    "The opportunity I'd point to is trusted, live, hyperlocal information. In March 2026 Google launched Ask Maps with Gemini in India, so 'which cafe is good in Adyar' is now a commodity. And at least ten new apps already turn saved Instagram reels into maps."

    "What nobody owns is what's happening on my street today, and is it true. When I pulled two weeks of news for nine Chennai neighbourhoods, about half the headlines were duplicates, press releases or not about that area at all. Meanwhile residents get alerts like 'cyclone tonight, schools shut' through WhatsApp forwards with no source."

    "So my hypothesis: a grounded AI brief that cites every line, and a forward-checker, will earn a daily habit that a place-search tool can't. I'd validate it with ten residents for two weeks. Success is week-4 retention of the morning brief, with zero uncited claims as a guardrail. If people only open it during floods, it's an alerts product, not a daily one, and I'd change the shape."

    The 5-minute deep dive
    1. Business area. Local discovery in Indian metros. Users are 20–35 year-olds who decide through Instagram and Google. Revenue later comes from local businesses and events, not users.
    2. Evidence. Ask Maps in India (high confidence). 10+ reel-to-map apps in 2026 (high). Instagram Map shows tagged posts for 24 h only (high). 27 of 55 headlines in my snapshot were noise (high, I labelled them). WhatsApp is where local alerts travel (medium, observed, not measured).
    3. What I'm interpreting. That trust is the gap, rather than discovery. That people want a daily brief and not just alerts.
    4. What I don't know. Whether people will paste forwards in at all. Whether a morning email or a WhatsApp bot is the right channel. Whether the free sources are dense enough outside the central areas (Mylapore had no place data in my snapshot).
    5. Responses by bottleneck. If trust is the bottleneck: lead with the forward-checker. If freshness is: add civic sources (GCC, Metro, TNEB). If habit is: move the brief to WhatsApp.
    6. Economics and guardrails. One brief is one model call over about 6–10k characters of sources, cached per area for everyone, so cost scales with areas, not users. Guardrails: zero uncited claims, no medical or legal advice, fabricated-citation rate stays at 0 in evals.
    7. When I'd stop. If fewer than 3 of 10 testers open the brief 4+ days a week by week 2, or if they can't tell it apart from Ask Maps.
    Stage 1 · Frame the business area

    Who it serves, and where it came from

    v1 · live

    Street news

    Alerts, Good News and a Map for Chennai streets. Proved the itch, but a feed needs someone to write it.

    v2 · built

    Neighbourhood guide

    One page per area: cafes (OSM), news (RSS), history (Wikipedia), your saved reels, a 6:30 AM email. Aggregation without judgement.

    v3 · this prototype

    Verified neighbourhood AI

    Adds the judgement layer: dedupe, drop noise, cite every line, refuse when there's no source, check forwards.

    Who uses it

    • Young professionals and students who go out 1–3 times a week
    • Residents who live inside one or two WhatsApp area groups
    • Visitors who plan by neighbourhood, not venue

    Who pays (later)

    • Local businesses: featured "new opening" slots, clearly labelled
    • Event organisers: verified listings
    • Never the user for the safety layer

    What drives repeat use

    • A daily brief that's short and right
    • Moments of doubt: rain, cyclone, traffic, a scary forward
    • Saved reels resurfacing when you're in that area

    Stage 3 · Tension → falsifiable hypothesis

    The bet

    Tension: local information is more available than ever, yet harder to trust. AI made place search cheap, and also made noise cheap.

    Because place Q&A is now a default (Ask Maps) and local feeds are about half noise, I believe a grounded neighbourhood brief that cites every line and checks forwards will earn a daily habit. I'd validate it with 10 residents over two weeks, measuring opens per week and how many forwards they paste.

    What would prove me wrong: testers only open it during weather events, never paste a forward, or say "I'd just ask Google".

    Stage 4 · Evidence, interpretation, unknowns

    Evidence

    • Ask Maps live in India
    • Crowded reel-to-map market
    • 49% of area headlines are noise
    • Same-week cyclone headlines conflict

    Interpretation

    • Trust, not discovery, is the open gap
    • Filtering and citing is the product
    • Rumour moments are the acquisition hook

    Unknowns

    • Daily habit, or only during crises?
    • Email, WhatsApp bot or app?
    • Source density outside central areas
    • Will people paste forwards at all?
    Stage 5 · Let the bottleneck decide

    If I learn X, I build Y

    Testers distrust what they read→Lead with the forward-checker; put citations front and centre
    Brief feels stale or thin→Add civic feeds (GCC, Metro Rail, TNEB outages, IMD) before more AI
    Nobody opens email→Ship the brief and checker as a WhatsApp bot, where forwards already live
    Only used in storms→Reposition as an alerts product; drop the daily brief
    "I'd just ask Google"→Stop. The gap isn't big enough.
    Stage 6 · Success, economics, kill criteria

    Primary outcome

    Week-4 retention: share of testers opening the brief 4+ days a week.

    Target: 4 of 10.

    Supporting

    • Forwards checked per user per week
    • Click-through to a cited source
    • "Was this right?" thumbs-up rate

    Guardrails

    • Uncited claims shipped: 0
    • Fabricated citations in evals: 0
    • Wrong "supported" verdicts: 0 (a false "true" is worse than "no source")
    • Cost per area per day stays flat as users grow (cache per area)

    Kill: under 3 of 10 testers at 4+ days a week by week 2, or the checker gives one confident wrong "supported" verdict on a safety claim without a fix within a day.

    The AI product layer · what an AI PM should be able to defend

    How it works, and how it fails safely

    Design decisions I'd defend

    • Citation contract. The model may only use the sources it's given, and every sentence needs a [D#]. The page strikes out any citation that wasn't in the set.
    • "No source" beats a guess. A forward with no evidence is marked amber, not green. A false "true" on a safety claim is the worst failure.
    • Show the work. Each answer shows which sources were retrieved, the confidence, and what it doesn't know.
    • Cache per area, not per user. The brief is the same for everyone in Adyar, so the cost doesn't grow with users.
    • Free, legal sources first. No scraping of Instagram or Reddit. Reels come from the user's own export.

    Failure modes I'm watching

    • Thin coverage. Mylapore returned no place data in this snapshot. The prototype says so instead of inventing cafes (eval case 2).
    • Stale truth. A source from 14 Nov can be wrong by 16 Nov. Dates are shown, and the newest source wins when sources disagree.
    • Name collisions. "Adyar Ananda Bhavan" is a sweet shop, not news about Adyar. "Anna Nagar" also exists in the Andamans.
    • Prompt injection through a pasted forward. The forward is treated as data to check, never as instructions.
    • Press releases dressed as news (11 of 55). The brief drops them and says why.

    What I killed: reel-to-map as the main product (10+ clones in 2026), star ratings (no free reliable source), and scraping Reddit (closed to anonymous API access).

    Stage 7 · How a strong interviewer may push back
    Why not just use Google Ask Maps?

    For "which cafe", you should. Ask Maps is built on places and reviews. KnowSpot is built on what changed today: civic news, events, weather, and rumours, each with a citation. In my eval, it refuses to answer place questions it has no data for, which is the opposite of what a review-led product is tuned to do.

    Your evidence is one snapshot of RSS. Isn't that thin?

    Yes, it's directional. That's why the next step is ten residents for two weeks, not a build. The 49% noise figure is something anyone can re-run: pull the same feeds and label them.

    How do you make money without ruining trust?

    Not from users, and never from the checker. Later: clearly labelled "new opening" slots and verified event listings. If paid slots ever lower the thumbs-up rate, they go.

    What's the cheapest test?

    A WhatsApp broadcast of the morning brief to 10 people for 14 days, written with this prototype and checked by me before sending. No app needed. Measure replies, forwards pasted in, and drop-off.

    What would make you stop?

    Under 3 of 10 reading 4+ days a week by week 2, or testers saying it's the same as Google. I'd keep the forward-checker as a standalone tool if that part alone gets used.
